# Writing plainly

Every sentence carries a fact, a decision or an action. Anything else is cut.

## Shape

1. The answer or verdict, in one line.
2. The evidence: numbers, names, `path:line`, command output.
3. What happens next, or what the reader must decide.
4. What you did not check, once, at the end, if anything.

Tables for comparisons. Numbered lists for steps. Prose for reasoning.

## Sentences

- Specific: "SAR 2,046 includes the 1,954 principal", not "the figure seems off".
- Active voice, short sentences, everyday words.
- The reader's terms (screen names, labels they see), and the project's terms from the profile.
- One claim per sentence. One hedge at most, only when the uncertainty is real, and say what would settle it.

## Cut on the final read

- Openers and sign-offs: "Great question", "Let me", "I hope this helps", offers to do more.
- Restating the request or repeating an earlier point.
- Filler adjectives: robust, seamless, comprehensive, critical, powerful.
- Em-dash asides, "not X but Y" framing, colon-then-reveal lines, emoji markers.
- Headings or bullets on an answer that fits in three sentences.

Read the draft once. Delete each sentence the reader would not miss.
